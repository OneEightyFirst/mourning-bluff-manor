### Room 000 - Entrance Hall

The entryway is a vast, two-story hall paneled from floor to ceiling in dark, heavily carved wood. Behind the players stand the two massive halves of the front entrance, each fitted with black iron hinges, a long brass handle, and narrow panes of glass. Directly ahead, a broad staircase rises beneath a pointed arch, its red carpet held in place by tarnished brass rods. The stairs divide at the upper landing and join a gallery that overlooks the hall through a row of narrow Gothic arches. Two elaborate brass candelabras stand on heavy sculpted bases at the foot of the staircase, each holding dozens of tall, unlit candles. Eight interior doors are arranged symmetrically around the hall. Three line the left wall and three line the right. The remaining pair face back into the entryway from beneath the staircase, one on either side, and appear to be matching closets built into the paneling. Each door stands within an ornate pointed arch and is made from the same dark wood as the walls, making the closed ones nearly disappear. Seven are shut. The farthest door along the left wall stands open onto a long Gothic gallery.  [The open door may be the only one that moves easily when the players first arrive. The others may be locked, stuck, or simply open onto different rooms when tried later. The two doors beneath the stairs may be ordinary closets, passages into the walls, or something else entirely.] An umbrella stand beside the entrance holds several black umbrellas, and a small reception table bears a silver calling-card tray, a handbell, and a guest ledger opened to a blank page. [The ledger already contains the visitors’ names on earlier pages, written in different inks and dated across many years.] Above the front doors hangs a large carved crest depicting a house beneath a gallows tree. The face of the clock set into its trunk has no hands, though a slow ticking can be heard somewhere in the hall. 

The gallary: Heavy ribs divide its vaulted ceiling into repeating arches, supported by carved wooden columns that continue into the distance. Tall leaded windows line the left side, their panes washed grey by the rain outside. Dim wall sconces repeat between the columns on both sides, lighting console tables, closed doors, and large paintings whose subjects cannot be made out from the entryway. Their light creates alternating bands of amber and shadow across the patterned wooden floor. At the far end, an arched doorway is barely visible through the darkness, much farther away than the dimensions of the house should allow.

*Lucius, You have told me every season for eleven years that the children are close, and every season I have chosen to believe you, because it was easier to trust you than to doubt them. #### The Basement

### Room 100 - Spiral Stair Room

A tall, narrow room rises through several stories of the house, its plaster walls bare except for faint horizontal marks where floors or galleries may once have joined them. A freestanding spiral staircase of black-painted metal occupies the center of the room, pierced iron steps winding tightly around a slender central column, climbing from the tiled floor toward a circular opening lost in the darkness above. - Rust freckles the undersides of the steps and the curling handrail, while the surfaces touched by hands and shoes have been worn smooth.
- Four doors stand at ground level, one in each wall, but there are no landings corresponding to them and no other visible entrances anywhere overhead.
- At the foot of the column, wedged into the narrow gap beneath the lowest curl of the stair, a second door is set flush into the tiled floor itself: a slab of pitted iron, scarred with old tool-marks, showing no handle, keyhole, or hinge from this side.

[Every account of this door, the Fellows' own papers included, insists it has never opened under its own power, through force, or through anything short of the ritual it was built for. There is exactly one exception. When a dark Major Arcana depicting the World is drawn at a Reader's table, the House Layer it creates may seize on this door specifically: the seam described below appears regardless of how many of the five objects have actually been gathered, and the door opens for as long as that Layer persists. Treat it as volatile and temporary, not as evidence the ritual's real requirements have changed.]

[Outside of that exception, the door does not answer to force, tools, or persuasion.
