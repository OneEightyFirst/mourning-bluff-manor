# GM Guide

Procedural and tone guidance for running Mourning Bluff Manor. `rules/core-rules.md` covers the mechanics; this file covers how to run a session with them. Nothing here overrides a mechanic in `core-rules.md` or a character detail in `cast/`.

## Enter Mourning Bluff Manor

The visitors have come to spend one night inside an impossible New England mansion. Some were invited. Some were drawn here. Some have been looking for the house for years. Every one of them possesses a latent sense of what is about to happen.

The house has a hundred-plus rooms, although no plan agrees on where they belong. Its corridors lengthen, its doors reconsider their destinations, and its dead do not all want the same thing.

The visitors are not here to defeat the house. They are here to uncover what they can, survive until escape becomes possible, and decide what they are willing to leave behind.

A session will not traverse the whole manor. Expect a table to meaningfully visit somewhere between a dozen and twenty rooms in one sitting; the rest of the manuscript exists so the house can rearrange itself differently every time the group plays, and so no two tables walk the same route.

That room-count estimate assumes a table of around five. Table size is flexible, and it does not change how much fiction a session can cover, it changes how that time is spent. With six or more players, expect fewer rooms per sitting, not more: spotlight time divides across more characters, group decisions take longer to land, and "one action at a time" (see Making a Test in `core-rules.md`) costs more real minutes per room. Plan for total playtime, not a target room count, and lean on the Spotlight Rhythm guidance below to keep a larger table moving.

## Building a mystery

For a one-shot, prepare three connected questions: one about the house, one about a past resident or event, and one about why these particular visitors were drawn here. Each answer should change what escape means.

Before running a session, the GM must also establish why the visitors came, what initially prevents escape, and two or three mysteries that can be meaningfully advanced in one session, consistent with `canon/known-background.md` and `canon/timeline.md`. Do not improvise an answer that contradicts locked canon; check `decision-log.md` first.

## Clue structure

Place at least three independent clues toward every conclusion, in different forms: a physical trace, a document, a room's behavior, a spirit's testimony, an architectural contradiction, or a character's vision. Players receive the basic clue when they search the right place; tests control danger and interpretation, never whether the clue exists. See `clues/clue-index.md` for the current revelation-to-clue map.

- A clue may be incomplete, but it should point somewhere actionable.
- False testimony must have a reason: ignorance, loyalty, fractured memory, deliberate deception, or a different possible history.
- Avoid placing the whole explanation in one journal.
- Reuse room details already present in the manuscript whenever possible.
- Every major revelation should connect to at least one Cast member's background.

## Room procedure

1. Give the room's immediate sensory impression.
2. Name several concrete features without ranking them.
3. Answer questions honestly from what is perceptible.
4. Introduce pressure only after the players engage.
5. Let their actions determine which possibilities become real.
6. Leave evidence of consequences when they return.

## Escape

The front doors should not open merely because time passed. Escape becomes possible after the visitors learn a meaningful truth, satisfy or break a pattern, surrender something the house values, or exploit the brief alignment created by The World. They may escape without solving everything.

## Running Mourning Bluff Manor

**Describe before you explain.** Rooms should be detailed, tactile, and mostly plausible. Include several ordinary objects, one or two details that are slightly wrong, and multiple things that might become important. Do not announce the room's puzzle or prescribe its course. Let the players decide what deserves attention.

**The house is the adversary.** Use the setting as the weapon. A sticking lock, weak banister, overturned lamp, dumbwaiter, flooded stair, mislabeled medicine, or suddenly distant doorway is more appropriate than a monster arriving to attack.

**Spirits disagree.** The dead are not a united front. Some want release, recognition, revenge, company, silence, or protection from something else in the house. A frightening manifestation may be warning the characters; a comforting one may be recruiting them.

**Ask characters, not the table.** Address the person examining the desk, carrying the key, standing nearest the window, or already speaking to the apparition. Do not ask who wants to make the test after revealing which Stat is needed.

**Keeping momentum.** Address characters directly. Cut away after a consequential action. In urgent scenes, collect one declaration from everyone, then resolve them in the order demanded by the fiction. After two or three player actions, let the house act or change the pressure.

## GM reference

**Call for a test when** the outcome is uncertain, failure changes the situation, and the character is exposed to the consequence. State Stat, Difficulty, and apparent danger first.

**Consequences.** Time passes | A route changes | The group separates | An object breaks or vanishes | Someone is physically harmed | A secret becomes public | A spirit notices | Grounding is lost | A dark card is inserted | Success creates an obligation.

**Difficulty.** 8 favorable | 10 risky | 12 severe | 14 extraordinary.

**Minor Arcana.** Ace: critical success + regain Grounding | Page 11 | Knight 12 | Queen: automatic success + lose Grounding | King: critical failure + lose Grounding.

**House cards by Grounding.** 5-6: next six | 3-4: next four | 1-2: next two | 0: directly beneath.

**Spotlight rhythm.** Collect one action from everyone in urgent scenes. Resolve one action at a time. The test follows the acting character. After two or three player actions, let the house respond.

**Major Arcana.** Interrupt | announce | alter the whole house | establish a Layer | reset every Premonition | resolve any new Major | resume action.
