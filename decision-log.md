# Decision Log

## Locked

- The secret fraternity is **the Fellows of the Rook**, commonly **the Fellows**.
- Its head is **Dr. Lucius Ward**, titled the **First Fellow**.
- The original manor owner is **Miriam Bellweather**.
- Miriam became despondent and threw herself from the cliff behind the manor in 1900; her body was never recovered.
- The haunting's physical center is **the Tower**, not the Black Well.
- The Tower predates every reliable record. No one knows who built it or why.
- Most of the Tower was demolished to build Mourning Bluff Manor, but its buried circular foundation survives beneath the house. The Fellows called this surviving chamber the **Lower Circle**.
- The GM maintains a factual Known Background from which clues and false accounts are derived.
- Test results are determined by the margin above the Difficulty: exceeding it by 3 or more is a clean success; meeting it or exceeding it by 1 or 2 is success with a consequence; any total below it is failure.
- The five Stats combine familiar attributes: Rapport covers charisma and social influence; Notice covers perception and awareness; Reason covers intelligence, knowledge, judgment, and willpower; Nerve covers stamina, pain tolerance, courage, and sanity; Vigor covers strength, dexterity, agility, coordination, and athletics.
- When more than one Stat honestly fits an obstacle, the player's described method determines which applies. A character may resist a mental command with Reason by recognizing and rejecting the intrusion or with Nerve by enduring its pressure through courage and raw determination.
- Physical harm is specific and fictional rather than tracked with points or boxes. An injury affects only actions it would reasonably hinder or prevent. Medical attention changes the fiction and requires a test only when meaningful danger or uncertainty remains. Grounding is the game's only formal attrition track.
- Tools provide broad fictional permission rather than narrow numerical bonuses. A Tool may be used in any way the object reasonably permits. Any unusual property listed in its description adds possibilities without restricting its ordinary uses.
- For abilities limited to once per room, a room functions as a scene. Returning later begins a new scene because the house and situation may have changed.
- Blind Reach has been removed. Characters use their visible Premonitions normally unless one of their own Gifts, Weaknesses, or other explicit rules changes how they interact with their deck.
- Players cannot withdraw from a declared test after hearing its Stat, Difficulty, and apparent danger; the former Withdrawing rule has been removed.
- Whenever a Major Arcana activates, establish its house-wide Layer and then reset every character's Premonition. Its owner discards the Major and reveals the next card; all other characters discard their current Premonitions unused and reveal new ones. Finish the reset before resolving any additional Major it reveals.
- The Cast contains ten available archetypes. Table size is flexible: each player chooses a different one, up to all ten. (Supersedes an earlier fixed-five assumption; the actual table this is designed for runs with seven.)
- **The Historical Archaeologist** replaces the Paranormal Investigator and the separate Archaeologist concept.
- Architectural Historian, Urban Explorer, and Former Resident have been removed from the Cast.
- **The Inheritor** replaces the Person Whose Name Appeared in a Document Written Before Their Birth.
- The Inheritor is an otherwise ordinary person who wants nothing to do with Mourning Bluff Manor. Their late mother was obsessed with the house; after her death, their father destroyed her research and refused to discuss it.
- The Inheritor is the Cast's strongest physical archetype, with Vigor +4. Their presentation remains gender-neutral.
- The Inheritor's Weakness, **Denial**, begins with the thought: "I cannot believe the supernatural is real; it cannot be real, or I will never sell this godforsaken house." When they willingly participate in an action intended to communicate with, obey, appease, summon, or banish a supernatural entity, they discard their visible Premonition and make their next test blindly.
- The Inheritor's Gift, **Clean Slate**, lets them shuffle their visible Premonition back into their remaining deck and reveal a new one once per room. The discard pile is not shuffled, and a newly revealed Major Arcana activates normally.
- The Inheritor's **Skeleton Key** fits many original interior locks and otherwise functions as an ordinary small metal key. It does not automatically open modern, blocked, combination, house-created, or altered locks.
- The Fraudulent Medium believes privately that they are a fraud despite their outward confidence. They once experienced a single apparently genuine vision of Mourning Bluff Manor and have entered seeking proof that it was real.
- The Psychiatrist was responsible for treating the last known person to enter Mourning Bluff Manor. The patient returned profoundly disturbed, and the Psychiatrist has entered to understand what happened to them.
- The Psychiatrist has Vigor +2, reflecting some physical capability without making it their defining strength.
- The Detective became obsessed while investigating disappearances connected to the manor, was removed from the case, and is now entering while officially on administrative leave.
- The Priest enters to determine whether Mourning Bluff Manor requires a blessing or more serious spiritual intervention, placing the protection of the living before judgment of the dead.
- The Estate Lawyer has been removed from the Cast and replaced by the **Paramedic**, the practical counterpart to the Priest within the Protector role.
- The Paramedic joined because one of the people entering the manor is their friend, but no specific character is selected and that relationship is not a mechanic. Once inside, the Paramedic considers every injured person their responsibility.
- The Paramedic has Nerve +3, Notice +1, Rapport +0, Reason +2, and Vigor +4, mirroring the Priest's Stat line with physical capability in place of social authority.
- The Paramedic's Weakness, **Do No Harm**, triggers when they fail a test made to provide medical aid to someone who has suffered physical harm. Instead of revealing their next Premonition normally, they examine the top three cards, place the lowest-valued card on top faceup, and put the other two on the bottom in any order. Ties are chosen by the player. Major Arcana count as lower than Minor Arcana and activate normally if selected.
- The Paramedic's Gift, **In Reserve**, lets them set aside one visible non-Major Premonition per room and use it for a later test while leaving their current Premonition in place.
- The Paramedic's **Medical Bag** contains ordinary emergency supplies and provides the fictional ability to perform appropriate first aid. It does not restore Grounding or cure supernatural conditions.
- The Psychic Researcher's Weakness, **No Baseline**, forces them to make a test blindly if they are the first character to test in a room. This encourages them to observe another character before committing themselves without adding punishment after a failed test or Grounding loss.
- The Psychic Researcher's Gift, **Expected Value**, lets them use a fixed test value of 8 without consuming their Premonition once per room. The fixed result has no suit or special card effect.
- The Psychic Researcher's Tool is an **Environmental Data Logger** that records physical changes accompanying apparently supernatural events without identifying their cause.
- The Psychiatrist's Weakness, **Fear of Isolation**, causes them to lose 1 Grounding whenever they fail a test while no other player character is present in the room.
- The Psychiatrist's Gift, **Reframe**, lets them change the suit of their visible Minor Arcana once per room without changing its value. The chosen suit applies to that test and any effect that refers to its suit.
- The Psychiatrist's **Microcassette Recorder** contains recordings of their final sessions with the patient. The recorded sessions may be replayed, rewound, compared with the house, or shared with others, and the functioning recorder can make new recordings. The patient's statements are fixed but may be mistaken, confused, deceptive, or accurate in ways that only make sense inside the manor.
- The Detective's Weakness, **Tunnel Vision**, prevents them from willingly leaving a room after failing a Notice test until they succeed at another test. If forced out, they play blindly until they succeed.
- The Detective's Gift, **Follow a Lead**, lets them reveal two possible Premonitions once per room, choose one, and place the other on the bottom of their deck.
- The Detective's Tool is a dependable **Flashlight** that illuminates ordinary darkness.
- The Priest has Nerve +0, Notice +2, Rapport +4, Reason +3, and Vigor +1, making them socially and intellectually capable but personally vulnerable to supernatural pressure.
- The Priest's Weakness, **Beyond Doctrine**, triggers when they confront a supernatural phenomenon with prayer, scripture, the crucifix, or a religious rite and it does not recognize or respond to the act. They take -3 on their next test involving that phenomenon.
- The Priest's Gift, **Sacrifice the Crucifix**, allows them to expend their crucifix once per game to prevent a revealed Major Arcana from activating and remove it from play.
- The Priest's **Crucifix** functions as a physical object and religious symbol whose effect depends on how it is used and what it means to the spirit confronting it. It provides no automatic numerical protection.
- The Dreamer's Weakness, **Dream Logic**, gives them remembered rules for rooms. Breaking one turns their remaining hand facedown and forces them to choose from it blindly until the room ends; the hand must still be exhausted normally.
- The Dreamer's Gift, **Possible Futures**, replaces a single visible Premonition with a private hand of five cards that must be exhausted before being refilled. Major Arcana and dark House cards remain dormant until revealed for play. House cards are inserted directly into the hand, cannot be discarded voluntarily, and must be played before the hand refills; an inserted House card becomes the Dreamer's next play while they are Unmoored. While Unmoored, the Dreamer shuffles and plays blindly from the hand.
- The Dreamer's Tool is an inaccurate but useful **Incomplete Map** assembled from their dreams.
- Each Cast member has a background, five Stats, one mechanical Weakness rooted in internal conflict, one deck-manipulating Gift, one investigative Tool, and a blank Secret created by the player. The Secret must be concealed from the group and capable of causing conflict if exposed.
- Every Weakness begins with one sentence explaining the character's internal motivation, followed by its explicit mechanical trigger and consequence.
- Every Gift begins with a short first-person inner thought in italics that explains the character's relationship to the ability, followed by its mechanical rules.
- After choosing the Cast members, the players designate an eligible character as the **Organizer** who assembled the group. The Paramedic instead joined because one of the people entering the manor is their friend, without designating that character mechanically. Any combination of the other eligible characters can serve as Organizer.
- The Historical Archaeologist received an anonymous box of previously unseen evidence establishing a new inheritor's ownership of Mourning Bluff Manor. The inheritor hired them to investigate and demonstrate that the manor is not haunted.
- The Historical Archaeologist's Weakness, **Leave It Intact**, forces their next test to be made blindly after they deliberately damage or destroy part of Mourning Bluff Manor or one of its contents. Accidental damage and damage imposed as a consequence do not trigger it unless causing that damage was the declared action.
- The Relative's Weakness, **Survivor's Guilt**, hides their Premonition whenever another character goes missing and keeps them drawing blindly until the group is reunited.
- The Relative's Gift, **Take My Future**, lets them exchange visible Premonitions with another character making a test once per room.
- The Relative's Tool is a family photograph that may subtly change in response to the group's actions.
- The Fraudulent Medium's Weakness, **Never Break Character**, hides all three visible cards when they admit they may be a fraud, retract a supernatural claim, or confess uncertainty. The cards remain hidden until another character knowingly acts on their supernatural advice.
- The Fraudulent Medium's Gift, **The Sight**, keeps the top three cards visible while allowing only the topmost card to be played.
- The Fraudulent Medium's Tool is a **Séance Bell** through which a willing spirit may answer a yes-or-no question. Spirits may lie or possess incomplete knowledge.

What limits it is purely fictional, the described action must plausibly change the outcome, and only one character leads a given test. Documented in `rules/core-rules.md`.

## Provisional

- Miriam built the manor after losing her husband and two children.
- Ward promised access to a possible future in which the children survived.
- Miriam financed expansions based on rooms seen in her dreams.
- Ward obtained the manor through a fraudulent or coercive will.
- The intelligence behind the haunting learned to access possible futures through clairvoyant experiments.

- **Superseded:** An earlier working theory had Ward dissolving into the house itself after the final experiment. Replaced by the version below, which the room manuscript's items and clues (Ward's diary in Room 097, especially) already support without needing changes.
- **Locked, per `canon/known-background.md`:** Ward and four other Fellows conducted the final experiment: five Readers, one ritual, meant to hold every possible future at once. Partway through, one of the other four Fellows realized it was actually letting something old and hostile through from within or beneath the Tower, and broke the circle deliberately to stop it. This didn't stop or complete the ritual, it froze it at the moment of interruption, unevenly. The intelligence never fully got through, and still needs clairvoyant minds to finish what it almost let in. **Resolved:** the Fellow who broke the circle is named **Edmund**, per his own doubts recorded in Ward's diary (Room 097). The date is locked to **6 November 1903**, seventeen years after Ward became First Fellow in 1886, in `canon/timeline.md`.
- **Locked:** Ward and the three other Fellows besides Edmund are still looping the frozen ritual, a century deep, now gaunt and half-transparent from the repetition. Edmund is not looping with them: breaking the circle killed him outright, and what's left is a skeleton still positioned at the edge of the circle where he fell, chalk smeared beneath him. The other four loop endlessly around the body without acknowledging it.
- **Locked:** the intelligence experiences past/present/future at once rather than linearly, which is why it can answer a question before it's asked (the spirit lamp's backstory) and why Edmund lost faith the ritual was producing foresight at all. It wants a clairvoyant mind (or several) to occupy permanently, since it has no fixed position in time of its own. No true name is recorded; the Fellows' own epithet for it was **the Unasked Question**.
- **Locked, deliberately unresolved by design:** why the anonymous sender gave the Historical Archaeologist the box. This stays a permanent mystery, not a gap to fill later.
- **Locked, GM-only, no rulebook change:** the Inheritor is the default/presumed Organizer. This isn't written into `cast/inheritor.md` or `cast/cast-index.md` (both are part of the published rulebook), it's a GM-facing expectation only: the Inheritor's existing "which person in the group did you personally ask to come, and why do you trust them?" prompt already points this way, and it's the cleanest read of "you invited or permitted the others to enter."
- Ward's diary in Room 097 (`669-gallows-hill-way.md`) is now three dated entries (14 October, 2 November, 6 November 1903): decoding "the answer" (a marginal line in a deceased Fellow named Cross's old notes) and tracking down the spirit lamp, which was present at a fictional 1881 Boston sitting (a Mrs. Ashworth's circle, on Boylston Street, run by a rival psychical research society) where it reportedly produced an unfed light that answered questions before they were asked, unsettling enough that the rival society's own members eventually resigned rather than sit with it again. Ward buys it off the one member still willing to sell, and closes the entry declaring all five now gathered and the circle complete, with only a date left to set. Edmund's doubts and Ward talking him down before the date is set; and the night itself. The spirit lamp (not the Rook, not the mirror) is the item this diary describes acquiring. Neither the Rook's placement in Room 003 nor the mirror's placement in Room 006 is the subject of this diary. The spellbook's matching line in Room 010 was updated to match: "A flame that once answered a question before it was asked, offered now for something else entirely."
- **The mirror moved**: it no longer hides behind furniture in Room 015. It sits in plain sight on the vanity in Room 006 (the Vanity Bedroom), among the perfume bottles, combs, and facedown photograph already there, the same vanity whose own built-in mirror was removed. It's easy to miss psychologically (players may have learned "there are no real mirrors here" by the time they reach it) rather than physically. Room 015 no longer holds any of the five ritual items.
- **Most easily missed of the five, by design intent**: the ceremonial dagger in Room 029 (Cabinet of Curiosities), buried among dozens of dissimilar small curios rather than standing out in a small, orderly set the way the mismatched Rook does in a 32-piece chess set.
- **Locked:** Three possible endings, each mapped to an existing Major Arcana already in `rules/core-rules.md`, and all three use the same five ritual items, gathered at the Lower Circle, in one of two opposed ways:
  - **(1) Sever, The Tower** ("violent restructuring exposes what was concealed as routes and certainties collapse"): the Cast destroys all five ritual items at the Lower Circle. This finishes what the Fellow who broke the circle started. Nothing is redeemed, the frozen ritual and the five Readers caught in it (Ward included) end with it, but the intelligence's channel through the Tower is closed. Cost is impersonal: the historical objects only, nothing personal is taken from the Cast.
  - **(2) Complete, The World** ("the house briefly aligns into a comprehensible whole"): the Cast finishes the ritual as it was originally meant to complete, using all five items correctly rather than destroying them. The house goes still. The Cast escapes successfully. But as they leave, they carry a settling, wordless certainty that something dark got out with them. This is a real win mechanically (they escape, the haunting ends) and a genuine loss narratively (they were the ones who let the sealed evil through, which is what the ritual was actually doing all along, per `canon/known-background.md`). **No additional mechanical cost** (an earlier idea to also consume a Cast member's Tool was considered and dropped): the dread of having let something out is the whole cost, deliberately, so it isn't disguised as strictly worse than Ending 1, it's a different kind of loss, not a bigger one.
  - **(3) False escape, The Moon** ("every falsehood contains a contradiction"): the default failure state, reached two ways. Either the table disengages from the Tower entirely and just heads for the front door, or they reach the Lower Circle but get it wrong: the wrong item destroyed or placed, or fewer than all five accounted for. Either path, the ritual stays frozen exactly as it was, and the house lets the Cast believe they've escaped anyway. One planted contradiction in that final scene is their only chance to catch the lie.
- **Working, not yet finalized:** A pentagram of five ritual components must be completed in the Lower Circle to trigger either ending 1 or 2. As a pacing tool, the GM may reveal that some points are already occupied (by traces of past victims, e.g. the 1988-89 research team from `canon/timeline.md`) to speed up a slow table. The spellbook describing the five, in period grimoire language without naming them outright, is written into Room 010 (The Still Library) in `669-gallows-hill-way.md`, a loose folio tied in black ribbon sitting on the reading desk.
- **Working, not yet finalized:** The five ritual components, one per Tarot suit plus one for the Major Arcana, each already placed in `669-gallows-hill-way.md`:
  - **Cups** (memory/grief): an ornate goblet carved from a single piece of polished wood, at the bride's place in Room 007, the Wedding Dining Room, the only piece at that setting made from anything but silver or glass.
  - **Pentacles** (the material house): an iron Rook, in Room 003, the Green Study, sitting on a chess table with a full ivory set otherwise. It's the only piece not carved from ivory, heavier and cruder than the rest, and doesn't match the set it's part of.
  - **Swords** (danger/truth): a ceremonial dagger in an unlocked case (the only unlocked one) in Room 029, the Cabinet of Curiosities. Every other item in the room has a handwritten catalog label; the dagger is the only one that doesn't.
  - **Wands** (fire/change): a physician's or chemist's spirit lamp, glass fuel bulb, wire cradle, brass wick collar, not an oil lantern, shelved among ordinary lanterns for no obvious reason, in Room 076, the Lamp Room.
  - **Spirit, outside the four suits** (Major Arcana): the one true mirror left in the house, sitting on the vanity in Room 006, the Vanity Bedroom (see above; moved here from Room 015).
  None of the five has any inherent mystical property. Each is an entirely ordinary object whose only notable quality is context: unlabeled, unmatched, out of place, or out of keeping with what surrounds it. Significance comes from the ritual and the spellbook's description, not from the object announcing itself. The earlier candidates (mourning hair, an empty urn as a *separate* item, a bell, a spirit photograph, a candle, a measuring instrument, and several now-removed magical properties on the final five) were considered and dropped, either for overlapping too closely with another item, for duplicating an existing Cast Tool (the Fraudulent Medium's Séance Bell), or for being self-evidently magical in a way that would let players identify the "special" item on sight instead of through investigation.

- **Locked:** the ceremonial dagger's provenance (Room 029, Cabinet of Curiosities). It was forged by a family of Romani smiths, travelers working through central Europe, who held it as an article of their trade that a blade meant to draw blood in a sanctioned killing must never be given a name, since naming it bound the smith's own soul to whatever the blade did afterward. Ward's own correspondence refers to the family only as "gypsies," his word, not the family's, consistent with how he treats Readers as instruments rather than people. He traced this specific blade through three further owners before buying it from a retired continental surgeon who agreed to sell only once Ward swore, in writing, never to ask what it had been used for. This confirms the dagger required real effort to find and acquire, same as the spirit lamp, consistent with Ward's diary saying "we now have them all" as of 14 October 1903. The dagger has a second, documentary clue to match the lamp's two-clue structure: a computer and dot-matrix printer in Room 093 (Computer Room), left behind by the 1988-89 research team, that power on under house influence and print a researcher's note transcribing a letter from Ward to an unnamed associate ("H.") describing the purchase. The note also explains, in-fiction, why the dagger (and everything else in Room 029) was never properly cataloged: the Cabinet of Curiosities doesn't hold a fixed inventory and its contents move on their own.
- **Locked:** Rooms 089-099 are no longer grouped as "basement," they're folded into the Service Wing section (1900s cellars and service infrastructure, still physically low in the house, just not framed as the uncanny basement). Room 093 was repurposed from Pump Room into the Computer Room to hold the dagger's documentary clue; Room 097 stays the Archive and keeps only the diary. Room 100 (Spiral Stair Room) is now the only room under its own "The Basement" heading, the sole true basement/underground mystery room tied to the Tower.
- **Locked, session 1 actual play:** the party reached Room 100 with only 2 of 5 items (Rook, mirror). A dark Major Arcana World card was drawn, and its House Layer seized the sealed iron floor-door, opening it regardless of the item count. This is a special, volatile exception to the five-item rule, not a change to the real requirement; the true ritual still needs all five. Beyond the door: a second, stone-cut spiral staircase (distinct from the black metal one above), ending in a circular stone room with a narrower stair spiraling down inside its own wall, and beneath that, the Lower Circle itself, directly under the Tower. As of session 1's end, this entire descent is still standing open (the dark card's Layer hasn't resolved/expired). GM judgment call for when, if ever, it reseals.
- **Locked, GM-only, for this table:** why the Cast was gathered this particular night. The Inheritor is the practical organizer, using their new, unwanted ownership of the manor as the pretext to call in people they know or trust, each for their own established reason (the Detective's obsession, the Paramedic's friend, the Psychiatrist's patient, the box that reached the Historical Archaeologist, and so on). Underneath that, GM-only, it isn't really coincidence: the house's own pull on precognitive minds (`canon/known-background.md`) is what let the Inheritor's invitation land on exactly this set of people at exactly this time.
