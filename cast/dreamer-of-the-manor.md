# The Dreamer

## Bio

You have walked the rooms of Mourning Bluff Manor in your dreams for months, recognizing people you have never met and always waking before you reach the same locked door. Recently the dreams changed: five visitors entered the house together, and one of them was you. When you discovered that an investigation was actually being organized, you contacted the others because you already knew where and when they would meet.

## What Is Established

The dreams began before you knew the manor or the investigation existed. They contain real details, but they are incomplete and may not obey waking logic. You chose to follow the dream into the house rather than wait for it to come true without you. Your name, age, gender, appearance, occupation, relationships, and personal history are yours unless established above.

## Make the Character Your Own

Your waking life is yours to define. You could be artistic, methodical, spiritual, skeptical, exhausted, or entirely ordinary. Decide whether you record the dreams, hide them, turn them into art, or treat them as a medical problem. You may believe the dreams are prophecy, trauma, coincidence, or a message from something inside the house. You do not have to decide which explanation is true.

## Decide Before Play

- What changed in your life when the dreams began?

- What harmless detail from the manor have you seen so often that it feels like home?

- What do you believe waits behind the locked door?

## Stats

**Nerve +3 | Notice +4 | Rapport +2 | Reason +1 | Vigor +0**

## Weakness — Dream Logic

I learned the house's rules in my dreams. Breaking them now feels like stepping off a path in the dark.

When you enter a room, the GM may tell you one simple rule you remember from your dreams.

If you knowingly break that rule, shuffle the cards remaining in your hand and place them facedown. For the rest of the room, choose cards from your hand blindly. If you exhaust the hand before the room ends, draw the next hand facedown as well.

When the room ends, you may look at the cards remaining in your hand again.

## Gift — Possible Futures

*Every choice has already happened somewhere. I can still choose the version I want.*

Draw five cards and keep them in a private hand. You do not have a visible Premonition between tests. When making a test, choose one card from your hand and reveal it.

Do not draw replacement cards individually. Only after every card in your hand has been played or discarded do you draw a new hand of five.

A Major Arcana remains dormant while it is in your hand. If you reveal one for a test, it activates before the test. After resolving it, set it aside and choose another card for the interrupted test.

A dark House card also remains dormant in your hand. It cannot be discarded voluntarily, and you must play it before drawing a new hand. When the House inserts a card into your deck, place it directly into your current hand without counting it toward your five cards. If you are Unmoored, the inserted House card becomes the next card you play.

If another character's ability refers to your visible Premonition, it may affect the card you have chosen after you reveal it. The Relative may exchange Premonitions with you at that point, and the Priest may prevent a Major Arcana you reveal from activating. When you Help with a test, choose and reveal a card from your hand as your committed card.

If you become Unmoored, shuffle your current hand and place it facedown. Choose cards from it blindly, and draw future hands facedown, until you recover Grounding.

## Tool — Incomplete Map

*You carry a roughly drawn map made by an inexperienced hand with colored markers on several sheets of paper taped together. The lines are uneven, labels crowd one another, and the whole thing has been folded and unfolded until the paper has softened and the tape has begun to split along the creases.*

You may consult it, annotate it, compare it with the house, show it to others, or use the physical map in any other reasonable way. When examining a visible exit, you may ask the GM where the map claims it should lead. The GM answers according to the map.

The map may be outdated, incomplete, or wrong, but it always gives a specific destination rather than no information at all.

## Secret

Create a Secret that your character must hide from the rest of the group. It should be something that could cause conflict if the others discovered it.
